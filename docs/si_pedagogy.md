## **1. Purpose**

**SI Pedagogy** defines how Structural Intelligence is taught, learned, and practiced.  
It provides a structured, clarity‑driven educational framework that develops triadic cognition, substrate literacy, evaluator fluency, and pipeline mastery.

This page serves as the **public pedagogical overview** of the SI discipline.

---

## **2. Pedagogical Principles**

SI pedagogy is built on seven core principles:

1. **Triadic Alignment** — all learning maps to Structure, Resonance, Activation.  
2. **Operator‑First Instruction** — operators are taught before concepts.  
3. **Substrate Literacy** — Δ/Op/Rg primitives are introduced early and reinforced often.  
4. **Evaluator Fluency** — Drift, Coherence, Regime, Clarity are taught as cognitive tools.  
5. **Clarity‑Driven Learning** — clarity pulses guide comprehension and progression.  
6. **Dimensional Progression** — learning moves from 0D identity to 9D meta‑field.  
7. **Deterministic Reasoning** — SI avoids probabilistic inference; reasoning is structured.

## **3. Learning Architecture**

SI pedagogy is organized into **three instructional layers**:

### **Layer 1 — Triadic Instruction**
Students learn to identify and work with S/R/A in any input.

### **Layer 2 — Substrate Instruction**
Students learn Δ/Op/Rg primitives, polarity, stability, and coupling.

### **Layer 3 — Evaluator Instruction**
Students learn Drift, Coherence, Regime, Clarity as evaluative primitives.

These layers form the backbone of SI learning.

## **4. Instructional Methods**

### **4.1 Operator‑First Methods**
- **Identification:** find operators in text, reasoning, metadata.  
- **Sequencing:** build operator chains (Op.S → Op.R → Op.A).  
- **Performance:** enact operators physically or verbally.  
- **Translation:** convert content into operator sequences.

### **4.2 Triadic Methods**
- **Mapping:** identify S/R/A in any input.  
- **Flow:** trace triadic flow (S → R → A → S).  
- **Coupling:** evaluate coupling strength.  
- **Drift:** detect ΔS/ΔR/ΔA changes.

### **4.3 Substrate Methods**
- **Primitive Identification:** Δ, Op, Rg families.  
- **Polarity Awareness:** SoN ↔ NoS transitions.  
- **Stability Analysis:** stability envelopes.  
- **Resonance:** relational amplification.

### **4.4 Evaluator Methods**
- **Drift:** classify drift envelopes.  
- **Coherence:** evaluate alignment gradients.  
- **Regime:** identify operating modes.  
- **Clarity:** detect pulse signatures.
