## What Is RTT?

Resonance-Time Theory describes systems in terms of how coherence is built, maintained, transformed, or lost across layers, operations, and timescales.

Its working language includes:

- fields
- operators
- resonance
- coherence
- regime-sensitive evolution
- relationships between structure, flow, and time

RTT is presented as an overlay language rather than a replacement for existing physics or computation.

Its purpose is to provide another way of describing relationships between processes that might otherwise be examined separately.

---

## A Minimal RTT Vocabulary

The following concepts provide enough orientation to begin navigating RTT material.

### Fields

RTT uses a triadic field vocabulary:

- **Scalar field (φ)** — baseline state or substrate condition
- **Vector field (V)** — directed flow or action
- **Resonance envelope (R)** — coherence and alignment between layers, locations, or agents

These fields provide a basic language for describing structure, activity, and coherence.

---

### Operators

RTT describes changes in its fields through operator families including:

- **Diffusion** — spreading state or smoothing gradients
- **Alignment** — movement toward preferred patterns or targets
- **Coupling** — linking fields so changes interact
- **Activation** — nonlinear responses, thresholds, or amplification
- **Stabilization** — damping, saturation, or control of runaway modes

Operators describe transformation.

They should not be treated as isolated labels without considering the regime in which they operate.

---

### Coherence

Coherence is a central quantity in RTT.

It concerns how well flows align with structures and how stable that alignment remains over time.

The resonance envelope **R** provides a way of representing that coherence and its evolution.

Within RTT-oriented reasoning, this makes coherence more than a descriptive preference. It becomes something that can be examined across changing states and operational regimes.

---

### Time and Regime

Time in RTT is not treated only as a clock.

It can also represent the sequence of updates produced under a selected operator regime.

Changing the operating regime can therefore change how coherence is:

- created
- maintained
- transformed
- degraded

This relationship between **resonance, time, and regime** is one of the important ideas to preserve when moving into later RTT material.

## Foundational Triads

RTT contains several triadic structures that organize different aspects of the framework.

Among the foundational structures are:

### Resonant-Time Triad

A local temporal signature involving:

**frequency • memory / relaxation time • quality / coherence**

This provides a way of describing a system's intrinsic rhythm of excitation, decay, and coherence.

### Frequency–Fluids–Forces

A triadic view connecting frequency, propagation through continuous media, and forces that bias or couple modes.

### Spin–Electro-field–Temperature

The **SET** field engine organizes spin, electric or electromagnetic effects, and temperature-related gradients as interacting influences within the RTT framework.

### Silence–Noise–Resonance

The **S-N-R** triad distinguishes:

- **Silence** — available but unexcited capacity
- **Noise** — incoherent or random excitation
- **Resonance** — coherent excitation

These structures are introduced here as navigational landmarks.

Their deeper definitions belong to the RTT source and associated modules.

## Dimensional Extension

RTT also provides **Dimensional Core Operators (DCOs)** as a lightweight scaffold for reasoning into higher-dimensional structures.

An important boundary is preserved:

DCOs provide operator-level definitions without requiring a particular geometry, ontology, cosmology, or physical interpretation.

That openness is intentional.

The Foundations tier should preserve it rather than converting dimensional notation into stronger claims than the underlying RTT material makes.

## Use with AI

RTT materials are designed to remain independently understandable while still preserving their relationship to the larger framework.

For longer AI-assisted sessions, declare the working context:

```text
rtt=1 | coherence=declared | drift=bounded | paradox=structural
```

Then preserve three boundaries:

1. **Source vs. interpretation**  
   Distinguish what RTT explicitly defines from what is being inferred or explored.

2. **Canon vs. extension**  
   Do not automatically promote an example, Store package, or experimental module into canonical RTT.

3. **Coherence vs. agreement**  
   Treat coherence as structural alignment within the working model, not simply as agreement among participants.

When uncertainty appears, return to the originating source or Spine rather than filling the gap through assumption.
