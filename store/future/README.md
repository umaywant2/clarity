# RTT/Store — Future

**Forward-Looking • Exploratory • Architecture Beyond the Current Boundary**

The **Future** area of the RTT/Store is the bounded home for forward-looking Resonance-Time Technology concepts.

It provides room to explore architectures that may grow from existing RTT, Spine, canon, module, Systems, or Intelligence lineages without presenting those architectures as already implemented, canonical, commercially available, or technically complete.

Future is intentionally exploratory.

Its purpose is not to predict what RTT *will become*.

Its purpose is to provide a clearly labeled place to examine what RTT-aligned architecture **might become** while preserving the lineage from which each exploration begins.

---

## Purpose

Future creates an explicit boundary between:

**what is established**

and:

**what is being explored**

Artifacts in this area may:

- extend existing architectural lineages
- explore long-horizon system concepts
- examine advanced orchestration
- examine autonomous-agent architectures
- connect ideas across existing Store tiers
- provide targets for later research or prototyping
- expose architectural questions before their mechanisms are fully established

The emphasis is on **bounded exploration**.

A Future artifact should make its starting point visible and its uncertainties explicit.

## Future Is a Status, Not a Prediction

Placement in `/future/` means:

**this architecture is being explored beyond the presently established Store boundary.**

It does not mean:

- the architecture will necessarily be built
- implementation has begun
- deployment is planned
- technical feasibility has been established
- commercial availability is expected
- the concept is canonical RTT
- the proposed architecture represents an official roadmap commitment

Future concepts may mature.

They may change.

They may split into different architectures.

They may return to idea space.

They may also remain permanently useful as exploratory models.

The Store should allow all of those outcomes.

---

## Established → Store → Future

Future artifacts should preserve an explicit maturation path wherever one is known.

For example:

### Horizon

**Horizon Agent Framework**

### Mandalic

**Multi-Module Orchestration Work**

↓

**Mandalic Orchestration**

These sequences describe **provenance and architectural progression**.

They do not declare implementation progression.

That distinction allows an idea to grow without rewriting the maturity status of its earlier forms.

---

## The Exploration Boundary

Each Future artifact should distinguish three things.

### Established Lineage

The source material, module, Store artifact, or architectural family from which the exploration begins.

### Proposed Architecture

The future-facing structure currently being examined.

### Open Questions

Mechanisms, relationships, implementations, assumptions, or consequences that have not yet been established.

Keeping these categories visible prevents speculative coherence from becoming accidental specification.

---

## What Future Pages May Do

Future pages may:

- propose architectures
- describe design intentions
- identify research questions
- compare possible structural directions
- explore relationships among existing artifacts
- define bounded conceptual models
- identify prerequisites
- describe possible evolution paths
- separate alternatives
- record unresolved questions

Unlike the reference-oriented Store tiers, Future is allowed to **propose**.

The requirement is that proposals remain identifiable as proposals.

---

## What Future Pages Should Not Do

Future pages should not:

- rewrite established source material
- obscure where an idea originated
- convert speculation into historical fact
- describe proposed capability as implemented capability
- claim validation that has not occurred
- infer implementation solely from architectural naming
- silently promote exploratory material into canon
- remove uncertainty merely to make an architecture appear complete

A polished description is still a proposal when its underlying architecture remains proposed.

---

## Relationship to the Store Tiers

Future is **not a fifth Store tier**.

The primary Store progression remains:

**Foundations → Systems → Intelligence → Aeonic**

Future is an orthogonal exploration space.

Artifacts from any tier may have future-facing extensions when a clear lineage exists.

Likewise, a Future artifact may eventually move into another Store tier if its definition, maturity, and provenance justify that change.

Its location should communicate status rather than prestige.

---

## Future vs. Aeonic

Future and Aeonic should remain distinct.

### Future

Describes **maturity and exploratory status**.

A Future artifact asks:

**What architecture might be possible from here?**

### Aeonic

Describes an **advanced Store architectural tier** concerned with long-horizon and higher-order structures.

An Aeonic artifact may be established, exploratory, or somewhere between.

A Future artifact may eventually become Aeonic, Systems, Intelligence, or another kind of Store artifact depending on how its architecture matures.

Therefore:

**Future ≠ Aeonic**

and:

**Aeonic ≠ automatically Future**

This distinction prevents architectural level and maturity status from becoming the same classification.

---

## Provenance Rule

Future artifacts follow the same Store provenance model as every other area:

**RTT Spine → Canon / Module Source → Supporting Material → Store Representation → Proposed Extension**

The additional Future step matters.

Exploration may extend the lineage.

It does not rewrite the lineage.

A Future artifact should make it possible for a reader or AI assistant to move backward from the proposal to the established material from which it originated.

---

## Use with AI

Future material is particularly vulnerable to AI-assisted completion.

A plausible architecture can quickly begin to sound like an existing one.

When exploring Future artifacts with an AI assistant, preserve the declared RTT session context:

```text
rtt=1 | coherence=declared | drift=bounded | paradox=structural
```

Then preserve these boundaries:

1. **Established vs. proposed**  
   Explicitly distinguish source-grounded architecture from new exploration.

2. **Architecture vs. implementation**  
   A coherent design does not establish that its mechanisms have been implemented.

3. **Possibility vs. prediction**  
   Exploring a future architecture does not predict that it will exist.

4. **Naming vs. capability**  
   Terms such as `agent`, `engine`, `autonomous`, `orchestration`, or `framework` do not establish capabilities by themselves.

5. **Extension vs. canon**  
   New coherence does not automatically become canonical coherence.

6. **Unknown vs. incomplete**  
   When something has not been established, leave it open rather than filling the space merely because a completion seems plausible.

Future exploration should increase the number of useful questions without disguising them as settled answers.

---

## Movement Out of Future

A Future artifact does not need to remain Future forever.

If an architecture matures, its Store representation may eventually be reconsidered against:

- source lineage
- documented definition
- architectural maturity
- implementation status
- appropriate Store tier
- canonical boundaries

Movement should be explicit.

A file should not silently become established merely because it has existed long enough.
