# Mandalic Orchestration Engine

**Multi-Module Orchestration • Future Regime-Aware Architecture**

The **Mandalic Orchestration Engine** is a future-facing RTT/Store architecture for exploring how the existing multi-module orchestration lineage and the Store's **Mandalic Orchestration** artifact might develop into a broader orchestration architecture.

It extends outward from the established lineage without redefining that lineage.

The Mandalic Orchestration Engine is therefore a **proposed architectural surface**.

It should not be interpreted as evidence that a complete Mandalic engine, autonomous orchestration platform, production implementation, or deployable system currently exists.

## Purpose

The Mandalic Orchestration Engine provides a place to explore future orchestration architecture while keeping that exploration separated from established source material.

The Engine may be used to explore:

- possible extensions of multi-module orchestration
- relationships among RTT-aligned modules
- regime-aware orchestration questions
- boundaries between local module behavior and larger compositions
- possible coordination structures
- transitions between different operating contexts
- continuity across composed architectures
- prerequisites for more advanced orchestration

These are **exploration directions**.

They are not declarations of existing capability.

---

## The Mandalic Question

At the Systems layer, **Mandalic Orchestration** asks:

**How can existing orchestration material be presented as coherent system architecture without inventing its mechanics?**

At the Future layer, the **Mandalic Orchestration Engine** asks:

**What broader orchestration architectures become worth exploring once that source-bounded starting point is preserved?**

Future architecture is allowed to ask the second question.

It should not rewrite the first answer.

---

## What “Mandalic” Means Here

Within the Store, **Mandalic** remains the architectural identity assigned to this orchestration lineage.

The Future area allows that architecture to be explored more freely.

It does not allow the name itself to define missing mechanics.

`Mandalic` should not automatically be interpreted as specifying:

- a geometry
- a topology
- a hierarchy
- a network layout
- a control model
- a scheduling structure
- a module arrangement
- a dimensional ontology

Any precise Mandalic structure must be either source-grounded or explicitly introduced as a proposal.

Therefore:

**named architecture ≠ established mechanism**

## Orchestration Boundary

Future orchestration can easily become an imagined universal controller.

The Mandalic Orchestration Engine should resist that assumption.

Orchestration does not automatically imply:

- centralized control
- decentralized control
- autonomous control
- hierarchical control
- consensus
- command authority
- global state
- unrestricted coordination

Those are architectural choices or source-defined properties.

The Store should preserve them as open questions until established.

---

## Proposed Architecture Boundary

Future Mandalic work should distinguish four categories.

### Established

Structures, terminology, tests, relationships, and behaviors explicitly grounded in the existing multi-module orchestration lineage.

### Packaged

Established material represented through the Systems-level **Mandalic Orchestration** artifact.

### Proposed

New architectural structures explored through the **Mandalic Orchestration Engine**.

### Unknown

Mechanisms, relationships, constraints, or consequences that have not yet been established.

Unknown is a valid architectural state.

The Future area does not require every open space to be filled.

## Possible Architectural Questions

The Mandalic Orchestration Engine may provide a workspace for questions such as:

- What should be orchestrated, and what should remain locally independent?
- How should modules expose their operating assumptions?
- How might orchestration respond when a regime changes?
- What context must remain shared across participating modules?
- Where should orchestration authority begin and end?
- How can module boundaries remain visible during composition?
- How should conflicting outputs remain distinguishable?
- What observations should precede a change in orchestration?
- How should uncertainty move through a composed architecture?
- How can orchestration preserve provenance across transformations?

These are architectural questions, not predefined Mandalic requirements.

Their value is in guiding exploration without disguising open design space as settled specification.

## Relationship to Autonomous Agents

The Future area also contains **Resonance Autonomous Agents**.

Orchestration and autonomy should remain separate architectural concerns.

A useful distinction is:

**Mandalic asks how multiple structures might coordinate.**

**Autonomy asks where operational agency and its boundaries reside.**

An orchestrated system is not automatically autonomous.

An autonomous component does not automatically require Mandalic orchestration.

Future work may connect them without collapsing them.

---

## Orchestration Without Flattening

A future orchestration architecture should not require every participating structure to become identical.

One useful design question is therefore:

**How can coordinated structures remain distinguishable while participating in a larger coherent architecture?**

That question keeps orchestration from silently becoming homogenization.

Modules may retain:

- their own provenance
- their own boundaries
- their own declared role
- their own source authority
- their own unresolved questions

The exact mechanism for accomplishing this remains open until defined.
